Additional Information — a draft, so the cost of using it is zero

Deferred by choice, and it cannot be filled afterwards. Here is a version in Raphael's own voice — deliberately plain, because an over-polished paragraph from a thirteen-year-old reads as parent-written and does more harm than good. Edit it, cut it, or ignore it:

I have grown up between two languages and two school systems. I started at a French-curriculum school in Hong Kong and moved to an American one in Grade 5, which meant learning to write and think differently halfway through primary school. Boarding is not a new idea in my family. Both of my grandmothers were headmistresses, my mother went to boarding school in Pennsylvania, and my sister is at Choate now, so I have been able to ask a lot of questions about what it is really like. Outside the classroom I referee football matches for younger children, which taught me to make decisions quickly and stand by them even when someone disagrees. This summer I taught trumpet to students at the Cambodian Children's Fund and helped collect instruments for them. I am applying because I want a school where I can keep doing all of these things at once.

Four short paragraphs, about 900 of the 6,000 characters. It answers the two questions every committee asks about an overseas thirteen-year-old — will he cope with the change, and does the family understand what boarding is — without ever stating them.
