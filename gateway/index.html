Explained The SCAT point, in plain terms

What the line says now: "SCAT High Honors (Advanced Verbal & Math)"

The SCAT has exactly two sections, and they are called Verbal and Quantitative. There is no section called "Math" — that is the everyday word for it, not the test's own. So the bracket currently uses a name the test does not use.

Separately, CTY grades results in levels: a qualifying student reaches either "CTY-Level" or the higher "Advanced CTY-Level". That is where the word "Advanced" comes from — it is a level, not a description of a section.

So the line is mixing two vocabularies. If Raphael reached Advanced CTY-Level, the strongest and most accurate wording is something like "SCAT High Honors — Advanced CTY-Level, Verbal & Quantitative". If he did not, then simply "SCAT High Honors (Verbal & Quantitative)".

Why it is worth thirty seconds: American admissions officers know CTY's vocabulary well — it is one of the few credentials on this application they can place instantly. Written in CTY's own words it reads as a real, gradable achievement; written as "Advanced Verbal & Math" it reads as an approximation, and the reader cannot tell how high the bar was. The fix: look at his CTY score report or award letter and copy the wording from it exactly.
